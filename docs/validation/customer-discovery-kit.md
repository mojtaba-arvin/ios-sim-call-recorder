# Customer discovery and paid-pilot kit

**Status:** prepared; no interviews, prototype tests, customer commitments or payments are claimed.  
**Decision rule:** do not select a segment from technical feasibility or public competitor marketing alone.

## Candidate segment to test first

Start with independent professionals/consultants who routinely need an auditable record of client instructions and commitments during mobile calls. Treat this as a hypothesis, not a selected target. Compare it against small sales/service teams and compliance-led organizations before choosing a segment.

## 20-minute problem interview

1. "Tell me about the last mobile call where you needed an accurate record afterward."
2. "What did you need to remember or prove? What happened when you could not?"
3. "How do you handle this today: notes, CRM, system recording, another phone, or a service?"
4. "What does the current workaround cost in time, money or risk?"
5. "Which calls are most important: customer, supplier, professional, personal, or internal?"
6. "Who decides whether a recording tool can be used, and who pays?"
7. "What recording notice and consent process is acceptable in your workflow?"
8. "What devices, carriers, plans and countries do your users actually use?"
9. "Would a separate app-owned VoIP call be acceptable, or must the user keep their existing SIM call?"
10. "What would make you reject this even if the recording worked?"

Avoid leading with the proposed solution. Ask for past behavior and concrete examples before showing a prototype.

## Concept test (after problem interview)

Present these as distinct concepts, not interchangeable implementations:

- Companion/import library: manages a recording the user obtained elsewhere; does not record the SIM call.
- Carrier conference bridge: may work only for verified carrier/device/plan combinations and requires a consented provider call leg.
- Accessory-led capture: depends on a tested accessory signal path.
- App-owned VoIP: a separate calling experience, not native SIM-call recording.

Ask the participant to rank them and explain the workflow they would actually adopt.

## Willingness-to-pay test

Do not ask only "Would you pay?". Present a specific pilot offer after the cost and legal constraints are known:

- Exact workflow and supported device/carrier tuple.
- Explicit list of unsupported cases.
- Per-user or per-minute price and monthly minimum.
- Retention, export, deletion and access controls.
- Pilot duration, participant count, success criteria and support boundary.
- A real purchase order, signed pilot agreement or paid invoice as evidence; verbal enthusiasm is not a paid commitment.

## Pilot acceptance

A paid pilot requires:
- A named buyer and accountable legal/privacy contact.
- Written authorization to test the exact call flow and jurisdiction.
- A named carrier/device/plan matrix.
- Agreed call volume, duration cap and recording retention.
- A signed paid commitment and measurable success criteria.
- A documented stop/rollback process if consent, merge or audio validation fails.

## Interview evidence record

For each interview, store an anonymized ID, segment, buyer/user role, country, call workflow, current workaround, quantified pain, objections, concept preference, price reaction, next action and consent to follow up. Do not put names, phone numbers, call recordings or confidential customer details in public issues.

## Outreach sequence

1. Ask for opt-in introductions from professional associations, existing business contacts or relevant communities.
2. Send the recruitment note only to people who have agreed to be contacted.
3. Do not scrape private contact details or send bulk unsolicited messages.
4. Schedule 5–8 problem interviews in one candidate segment.
5. Review evidence and objections before changing product scope.
6. Offer a paid pilot only after physical, legal and technical gates pass.
